Why RealChart is free — and how we keep it that way

No subscriptions, no paywalled indicators, no locked timeframes. Here's the straightforward model that lets us give the charts away.

Published 11 November 2026 · RealChart

Most charting platforms make money by charging you a monthly fee. The free tier is deliberately limited — a couple of indicators per chart, a handful of alerts, delayed data — and the useful features sit behind a subscription. RealChart takes a different approach: the charting is free for everyone.

What "free" actually means here

  • No account required to use the charts. (You only sign in if you want price alerts saved to your profile.)
  • No paywalled indicators. All 100+ indicators are available to everyone.
  • No locked timeframes and no artificial limits on drawing tools or watchlists.
  • No charge, ever, for the core charting experience.

So how does it support itself?

Running a charting site has real costs — market data and servers aren't free. Rather than charge users, RealChart earns through optional broker partnerships. If you're outside the UK and decide you want to trade with real money, you can choose to open an account with a partner broker through RealChart, and the broker pays us a referral fee. That's entirely optional and completely separate from using the charts.

Crucially, you never have to open an account, deposit money or trade to use RealChart. The partnership simply means the people who do choose to trade help keep the charts free for everyone else.

What we don't do

We don't sell your personal data, and we don't bombard the charts with ads. The priority is a clean, fast tool you'll actually want to use. Prices shown are for information; RealChart doesn't give investment advice, and it isn't a broker itself.

A note for UK visitors

In the UK, RealChart shows live prices for information only — there's no account-opening offer, because the partner broker isn't authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority. You get the full charting experience; you just won't be asked to open an account.
